AI Tools for Business in Kathmandu: Honest Picks

Almost every article about AI tools is written for a Silicon Valley headcount with clean data. A business in Kathmandu is different — curation, power cuts, paper-first processes and low-data environments. Here is what survives contact with a Nepali office.

NATDS Field Notes · Aug 2026 · 6 min read

What actually gets adopted

  • Document & PDF knowledge Q&A. Teams switch from "where is that clause?" to "ask your documents" instantly. This is AI for business at its most honest.
  • Meeting summarisation. A 90-minute board meeting becomes a one-pager the same evening. Adoption is instant.
  • Decision briefs with evidence. Instead of forwarding six WhatsApp PDFs, a manager gets one page of options, each tied to a source.
  • Low-code automations for repetitive email and spreadsheet work — no engineers needed on day one.

What quietly fails

Generative dashboards and "copilots that replace analysts" tend to fail in low-data environments. If the underlying data lives in paper files, PDFs and tribal memory, a fancy copilot only hallucinates confidence. Start with a small, real workflow — ideally the one that eats the most Friday-afternoon hours — and make that work.

The Kathmandu rule

Treat the tool as the last decision, not the first. First map the workflow, then pick the smallest tool that closes it, then automate the follow-up. That is how AI at Kathmandu companies moves from demo to habit — and it is exactly the sequence we teach in our programs for managers and teams.
