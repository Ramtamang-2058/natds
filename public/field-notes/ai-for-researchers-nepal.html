AI for Researchers in Nepal: From Literature to Citations

AI for researchers is not about letting a model write the paper. It is about doing the slow parts of research faster — reading the literature, synthesising it, verifying every claim, and writing with citation discipline.

NATDS Field Notes · Aug 2026 · 5 min read

The researcher's pipeline, reworked

Academics, thesis writers and research teams in Nepal hit the same wall: the literature is published globally, the time is local, and the citation expectations are unforgiving. The Researcher Program teaches a disciplined pipeline:

  • Literature mining across Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar and Elicit.
  • Deep reading and synthesis with NotebookLM, GPT and Claude — with the researcher in charge of structure.
  • Verification — every claim traceable to a source, using research-grounded answer engines.
  • Knowledge management — Zotero, Obsidian and connected notes the researcher keeps forever.
  • Citation-aware academic writing — AI-assisted drafting, not AI-authored conclusions.

Why this matters specifically in Nepal

Nepali researchers often carry heavy teaching loads while supervising multiple theses. AI collapses the infrastructure work of scholarship — the searching, the reading backlog, the formatting — so the human spends the saved hours on analysis and argument. That is the honest version of AI research in Nepal: more rigour, not less.

Verification is non-negotiable. A source that cannot be checked is not a source, whatever the model claims. Every claim in this program leaves a trail a supervisor can follow.
