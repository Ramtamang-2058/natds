Practical AI Training in Kathmandu: A Local Guide

Kathmandu has no shortage of AI demos. Practical training is different: you leave with one workflow you have actually built, tested and agreed to use.

NATDS Field Notes · Nov 2026 · 6 min read

Teams in Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bhaktapur often ask the same question: should we send people to a public course, bring a trainer to the office, or join live online? The answer depends on what you want people to do with AI on Monday morning.

What "practical" should mean in the room

A practical session does not need a technical audience. It does need real material. Participants should bring a laptop, a Google account and one non-conflicting, non-sensitive recurring task — a report, a set of notes, a stack of documents. Remove staff, customer and commercially sensitive details before the session.

In our sessions the work is explicit:

  • Map a real week into work to keep, delegate, automate and stop.
  • Build a reusable prompt library for the tasks that repeat.
  • Turn a sample document set into a question-and-answer workflow.
  • Write one decision brief with evidence that a colleague can check.
  • Agree what may be uploaded, who reviews output and how AI use is recorded.

That last point matters in Kathmandu offices where the same files move between finance, operations and leadership. A tool is only useful if the rules for using it are clear.

Classroom, live online or at your office

Classroom training suits individuals or small teams who can travel to a venue in the valley. It is good for shared exercises and for meeting other teams facing similar problems.

Live online training follows the same curriculum with breaks and exercises. It works well when participants are spread across branches, or when travel across the valley would take more time than the session itself.

In-house training is delivered at your office. It is the strongest option for a department rollout because the examples come from your own work and the whole team learns the same rules at once. Travel within Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bhaktapur can be arranged as part of the engagement.

A sample session pattern

Eight two-hour sessions over several weeks is a useful shape. The first session maps the work. The middle sessions build the workflows. The final session turns them into a 30-day plan with owners. The gap between sessions is not wasted time — it is when people try the workflow at their desk and bring back what broke.

Questions to ask a provider in Kathmandu

  • Can we work on our own non-confidential examples?
  • What will each participant be able to do after the last session?
  • How do you teach review, data handling and disclosure?
  • What support is available after training?

If the answer is only a list of tool names, ask for a sample exercise instead.

Frequently asked questions

Where can training happen? In a classroom, live online, or at your office across Kathmandu, Lalitpur and Bhaktapur.

What should participants bring? A laptop, a Google account, and one non-sensitive recurring task from their own work.

Is one day enough? A day introduces tools. Several sessions with time between them are needed to change how a team works.
