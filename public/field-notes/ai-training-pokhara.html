AI Training in Pokhara: On-site, Live Online and Hybrid

Teams in Pokhara do not need a different curriculum from Kathmandu. They need a delivery format that respects distance, branch schedules and the work actually sitting on people's desks.

NATDS Field Notes · Nov 2026 · 6 min read

Searches for AI courses in Pokhara usually lead to national providers who only deliver in the capital. NATDS works differently: the same practical curriculum can be delivered on-site in Pokhara, live online, or as a hybrid with some sessions together and some remote.

Three delivery options

On-site in Pokhara. A trainer joins your team at your office. This works best for a whole department, because everyone works through the same non-confidential examples and agrees on the same rules for review and data handling.

Live online. The same sessions, with exercises and breaks, delivered to participants in Pokhara and other cities at once. This is useful when a team has branches or when travel time would eat into the session.

Hybrid. One opening and one closing session on-site, with the build sessions online. The team gets a shared start and a shared plan without needing everyone in the same room every week.

A realistic two-week plan

  1. Session 1 — map the work. List recurring reports, documents and decisions. Pick one workflow to improve.
  2. Session 2 — build the first version. Create a prompt library and a document question-and-answer workflow on a safe sample.
  3. Session 3 — add review. Define what data may be shared, who checks output, and how AI use is recorded.
  4. Session 4 — hand it over. Turn the workflow into a 30-day plan with an owner and a measure, such as time spent on a weekly report.

Eight two-hour sessions over several weeks works well for most teams. The time between sessions is when people test the workflow at work and bring back what failed.

What to prepare before the trainer arrives

  • A laptop for each participant and a reliable connection.
  • Group size, preferred dates and whether sessions should be on-site or online.
  • One recurring task with confidential details removed.
  • A decision on who owns the workflow after training.

We ask for non-confidential examples on purpose. The point is to learn the method, not to upload real staff, customer or financial records into a tool during a practice session.

Keeping momentum after the course

After training, pick one measure and check it two weeks later. Did the monthly report take less time? Did meeting action lists go out the same day? Can a new team member run the workflow without help? If the answer is yes, the training worked. If not, revisit the workflow rather than buying another tool.
