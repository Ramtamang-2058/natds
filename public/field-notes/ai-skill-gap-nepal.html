The AI Skill Gap in Nepal Is an Operations Gap

Every few weeks another "AI skills" event opens with a promise and closes with a flight-of-fancy demo. The gap in Nepali organisations is rarely a shortage of tools. It is a shortage of workflows with enough discipline to use them.

NATDS Field Notes · Jul 2026 · 4 min read

Tooling is not the bottleneck

In 2026, an SME in Kathmandu has the same frontier models available as an SME in Singapore, at the same price. What differs is the habit layer: who owns the prompt library, who checks the evidence, who is allowed to paste what into which tool. Without that layer, tools become toys.

Training, not tooling

Effective AI training in Nepal therefore looks like operations consulting wearing an education hat:

  • Map the real workflow first — where the hours actually go.
  • Pick one measurable win and automate it before expanding.
  • Write the policy — security, data-leak and bias guardrails, on one page.
  • Hand the system over — documented, so no single person is the lock.

Where to start

Start with the team that owns documents and decisions — typically managers and ops staff. Equip them first, because their output leaks efficiency to every other team.
