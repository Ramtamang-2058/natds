Corporate AI Training in Nepal: A Team Rollout Guide

Corporate AI training is not a lecture with a certificate at the end. It is a rollout: one workflow, one rule set, one team that can run it without you.

NATDS Field Notes · Nov 2026 · 6 min read

Nepali organisations often ask for "AI training for everyone". That is the wrong starting point. Everyone does not need the same exercise. A finance team, a research unit and a group of managers share the same principles, but they should leave with different workflows.

Step 1: pick one workflow

Choose a task that repeats and costs time: a monthly report, meeting follow-ups, a document people keep asking about, or a first draft of routine correspondence. Use non-confidential examples in the room. Do not try to transform the whole department in week one.

Step 2: train managers first

Managers set the habits everyone else follows. Our AI for Managers & Executives program is eight two-hour sessions built around a real management week: decision briefs, document Q&A, meeting notes, a reusable prompt library and a simple policy. Once leaders use the same review discipline, the rest of the rollout is much easier.

Step 3: write a small use policy

A one-page rule set is enough to start:

  • Which documents may be uploaded to a tool, and which may not.
  • Who checks an AI output before it is shared.
  • How AI use is recorded where a client or committee needs disclosure.
  • What happens when an output is wrong.

This is not bureaucracy. It is what lets a team move quickly without guessing.

Step 4: measure and extend

Pick one number before training and check it two weeks later. Did the report take less time? Did action lists go out the same day? Can a new colleague run the workflow? If the answer is yes, extend to the next workflow. If not, fix the first one before adding another tool.

What we have seen in Nepal

NATDS has trained IME Group staff with certification and delivered on-site training for Prakriti Resource Center. The pattern is consistent: teams improve fastest when the training uses their own work and when a manager owns the follow-up. We do not claim engagements we cannot show.

Frequently asked questions

Who should be trained first? Managers and team leads, because they set the review habits the team follows.

How long does a rollout take? Eight sessions over several weeks, with time between sessions to try the workflow at work.

What should we measure? One number, chosen before training, checked again two weeks later.
